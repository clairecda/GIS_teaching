# Week 1 Lecture · Foundations of GIS

## This week
We introduce GIS as a way to connect location with descriptive data. Learners practise spatial thinking, explore QGIS for the first time, and understand how the course progresses from hands-on mapping to scripted analysis.

## By the end of the week you will
- Explain what GIS is and why geographic context matters.
- Identify the difference between vector and raster data models.
- Read basic metadata (CRS, extent, resolution) and note how it affects maps.
- Navigate the QGIS interface and produce a simple export from Natural Earth data.
- Articulate why data categories, boundaries, and map choices reflect priorities—not neutral facts.

## Key vocabulary
GIS · spatial data · attribute table · vector (point, line, polygon) · raster · metadata · coordinate reference system (CRS).

## Maps as choices

Before diving into software, consider this framing you can carry through all semester:

> **GIS doesn't just map the world—it reflects how the world has been understood, governed, and valued.**

Every dataset answers some questions very well—and makes others almost impossible to ask.

When you see a map of "crime rates," ask: Who decided what counts as crime? Which incidents get reported? How were boundaries drawn?

When you see a map of "land use," ask: Whose categories are these? What doesn't fit neatly into the boxes? What was here before these classifications existed?

This isn't about being cynical—it's about being a rigorous analyst. Professionals who understand how data is *produced*, not just how to visualize it, ask sharper questions and avoid hidden pitfalls.

**The goal of this course:** We won't tell you what to think politically. We'll teach you to *see assumptions*—in data, in boundaries, in classification schemes. That skill makes you a better GIS practitioner and a more thoughtful professional.

## What happens in class
- Brief discussion: "What do maps leave out?"—establishes the critical lens for the course.
- Contrast spatial vs tabular thinking with everyday examples (navigation apps, weather maps).
- Tour the QGIS interface and show where metadata lives.
- Demonstrate how to load and style Natural Earth layers, setting up the Week 1 lab.
- Outline the lightweight workflow: acquire → inspect → clean → analyse → communicate.
